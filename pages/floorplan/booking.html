Booking bookable space

HE-GN-04, revision 2. How project rooms, quiet rooms and hotelling desks are reserved in a Halverd managed building, and what a booking obliges you to.

What is bookable

Bookable space is space with no permanent occupant. The space classification standard sets out which types qualify: project rooms, quiet rooms, and any desk position recorded as hotelling rather than allocated. Everything else on a level is allocated space, and allocated space is never bookable, however empty it looks on the day. If you need allocated space for a meeting, ask the occupant, not Estates.

Whether a room is bookable is a property of its space record, not of the sheet. Departmental tint on the plan is advisory and is refreshed only when a sheet is reissued, so a room that reads as shared on an older sheet may have been reallocated since. The space record governs.

Making a booking

  • Open the space record from the plan and check that the space type is one of the bookable types. The record also carries the extension for the space, which is what a visitor will be given.
  • Place the booking with the Estates service desk, quoting the building, the level, the room code and the slot. The console shows whether a space is bookable; the booking diary itself is held by the desk.
  • Quiet rooms book in fifteen minute slots, to a maximum of two hours in a day per person. Project rooms book in half days. Hotelling desks book for a whole day.
  • Bookings open fourteen days ahead for quiet rooms and desks, and a quarter ahead for project rooms. A project room held for longer than a quarter is a fit-out request, not a booking, and goes through the move ticket queue.
  • Repeat bookings are set up by the Estates helpdesk in the same way, and are reviewed at each quarterly reissue.

Releasing and no-shows

Release a booking you no longer need. A quiet room unoccupied fifteen minutes into its slot is released automatically and the remainder of the slot returns to the pool; a project room unoccupied half an hour into a half day is released the same way. Three unreleased no-shows in a quarter suspend the ability to book for the rest of it, and the suspension is lifted by the Estates helpdesk once the reason is understood.

Furniture, plant and cabling

Take nothing out of a bookable room. Chairs, sheet rails, screens and floor boxes are recorded against the room and are counted at each sheet reissue. If a booking needs power that the room does not have, raise a move ticket for a power spur and book the room for a date after the work completes; the helpdesk will not accept a ticket for the day of the booking.

Occupancy reporting

Bookings feed the occupancy figures Estates reports quarterly, so an accurate record matters beyond the day. Two habits do most of the damage: booking a room and working elsewhere, and holding a project room across a quarter to keep other teams out of it. Both read as demand the portfolio does not have.

Where bookings are not held

This console does not hold bookings for parking, for the loading bay, or for the ground floor meeting suite, which carries catering and visitor access alongside the room. Those go to the building's own reception desk.
