Archive and records

The Institute archive occupies the two rooms behind the old chart store and is looked after by the Archivist. It holds the Institute's own paper: minute books of the governing body, the annual reports, the complete run of the Ferncliff Technical Notes, station logs and tide sheets from the founding of the field station onward, the specimen registers kept by the Materials Lab, and the staff rosters for every programme year before those published on this site.

Consulting the archive

The archive is open to Institute members and to outside researchers by appointment, ordinarily on Tuesday and Thursday afternoons in term. Write to the Archivist with what you are looking for and roughly when you would like to come; a first visit usually needs a week's notice, because most of the older material is boxed off site at the Trust's store and comes across by van. The reading room seats four. Pencils only, no food or drink, and no loose paper of your own beyond a notebook.

Asking for a copy instead

Much of what people want can be answered without a visit. The Archivist will scan a short extract, an entry, or a single sheet and send it by electronic mail, normally within ten working days; longer copying is quoted for, and the charge covers the handling rather than the material. Say what you need it for, because permission to reproduce anything in print is a separate matter and is given by the Office of the Registrar.

Closed material

Personnel files, appointment papers, grant applications and anything containing the medical or financial affairs of a named person are closed for seventy-five years. This does not affect the staff rosters, which were published documents from the day they were compiled and are open without restriction. Where a box holds both open and closed papers the Archivist produces the open part and lists what has been withheld.

Citing what you find

Cite Institute records by the title on the document, the programme year, and the Institute as holder, followed by the box and item number the Archivist gives you. A citation without the box number cannot be checked by the next reader, and the Archivist will supply one for anything you have already consulted if you ask.
