About the staff register

The staff register is the Institute's own record of who holds an appointment here. It exists because a coastal field station is a small place with a long memory: work published under the Institute's name is attributed to the people listed in the register for the programme year in which it was done, and the register is the document a later reader is expected to consult. The annual roster is the published form of the register.

What an entry represents

An entry records an appointment, not a person's whole relationship with the Institute. Anyone holding an appointment on the day of compilation appears, whatever their hours and whatever the length of their contract; anyone whose appointment had ended by that morning does not, even where they were still working out a notice period. The working title in an entry is the title held on that day, which is not always the title in the establishment record and is not always the title on a person's own correspondence. Where the two differ the register follows the working title, because that is what colleagues and correspondents will recognise.

Who is left out

Three groups are deliberately absent. Visiting scholars hold guest arrangements rather than appointments and are recorded in the annual report instead. Contracted trades, the boatyard among them, work to a service agreement and are listed with the estate records. Members of the Ferncliff Trust who take no salaried post at the Institute appear in the Trust's own papers.

How a roster is compiled

Data Services maintains the personnel system of record and extracts the register from it on the first working day of September. The extract is read against the establishment record by the Office of the Registrar, queried with each head of department, and then set for publication. Arrangement is settled year by year rather than fixed: some years the roster is grouped by department, which suits a reader looking for a whole team, and other years it is arranged alphabetically across the whole Institute, which suits a reader looking for one person. Either arrangement carries the same appointments.

Why rosters are not revised

A published roster stands as the record of a particular morning. Amending it afterwards would leave two documents bearing the same date and no way to tell which one a citation meant, so the Registrar carries every correction forward into the following year's roster instead. Anyone who needs a matter put right should read corrections and enquiries; anyone who needs a roster from an earlier programme year should read archive and records.
