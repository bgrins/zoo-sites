Water Quality

Source and Treatment

Grelsby draws its supply from two sources. The Sumac Row wellfield yields the greater share through four gravel-packed wells finished in the valley aquifer, and the Pumphouse Hill impoundment supplies the balance under a borough water right dating from the original 1911 ordinance. The two sources blend at the treatment works before entering the distribution system.

Treatment is conventional: aeration for dissolved iron and manganese, coagulation and filtration for turbidity, corrosion control by pH adjustment, and free chlorine for residual disinfection through the mains. Certified operators staff the plant on two shifts, with an on-call operator overnight.

Monitoring

Samples are drawn on a fixed schedule from the plant tap and from twenty-two points across the distribution loops, so that a result reflects what reaches a kitchen tap rather than what leaves the works. Results are reported to the state drinking water program and retained at the Works Yard office for public inspection during counter hours.

Bacteriological Weekly, rotating through the distribution sample points.
Disinfectant residual Daily at the plant and at three loop extremities.
Lead and copper Every third year at the tiered sites required by the program, drawn as first-flow samples by the occupant.
Disinfection by-products Quarterly at the two locations of highest residence time.
Inorganics and nitrate Annually at the entry point to the distribution system.

Annual Report

The Authority publishes a consumer confidence report each year covering the prior calendar year's monitoring, the detected substances and their ranges, and any violation or corrective action. Copies are mailed with the first bill of the second quarter and are held at the counter. A copy will be posted to any account holder on request to customer service.

Discolored or Cloudy Water

Rust-colored water almost always follows a change in flow — hydrant flushing, a main break, or heavy draw from a single hydrant — which lifts settled iron out of the pipe wall. Run the cold tap nearest the service entry until the water clears, and hold off laundry until it does.

Milky or cloudy water is usually entrained air, which clears from the bottom of a glass upward within a minute or two. Water that does not clear after ten minutes of running, or that carries an unfamiliar taste or odor, should be reported to the distribution office through customer service on (802) 555-0122.

Service Lines and Household Plumbing

The Authority owns the main, the corporation stop and the service line to the curb; the customer owns the line from the curb into the building, along with the interior plumbing. Most lead exposure in older housing comes from that customer-owned portion or from solder in the building, not from the mains, which is why lead and copper sampling is done at the tap.

Occupants of dwellings built before the borough's solder ordinance should flush a fixture that has stood unused overnight, use cold water for drinking and cooking, and clean tap aerators periodically. The distribution office will identify the material of the Authority-owned side of a service line from the tap card at no charge.
