Security and data handling

How your code is stored, who inside Bellhollow can reach it, and how to report a weakness
About Plans Security
Where your repositories live

Repository data is held in two leased facilities in the same country, one primary and one standby, and never leaves that country. Volumes are encrypted at rest, transport is TLS only, and plain HTTP is refused rather than redirected. Backups are taken hourly, kept for thirty-five days, and restored into an isolated environment once a quarter so that a restore is something we have practised rather than something we believe in.

Pipeline runners are the exception worth knowing about. A hosted runner is a fresh container that is destroyed when the job ends, and the workspace it built in is destroyed with it, but a job you wrote can send anything it can read to anywhere it can reach. Treat a pipeline definition as production access, and prefer a self-hosted runner for work that must stay on your own network.

Who can reach your code

Inside your organisation, access follows the repository's own permissions. A private repository is invisible to accounts that have not been granted it, an internal repository is readable by every member of the organisation, and a public repository is readable by anyone. A pull request inherits the visibility of the repository it targets, and so does its job log, which is why we advise against printing secrets into build output.

Inside Bellhollow, no engineer has standing access to customer repository contents. Access is granted per incident, against a support conversation you opened, for a maximum of eight hours, and it is recorded in your organisation's audit log where you can read it. Nobody at Bellhollow can grant themselves that access silently, and we do not use customer code to train anything.

Account security

Two-factor authentication is available on every plan and can be required across an organisation from its settings. Passwords are hashed with a memory-hard function and checked against a list of known-breached credentials at the moment they are set. Access tokens are scoped, expire on a date you choose, and are shown once; a token seen in a public repository is revoked automatically and the owner is told which repository it appeared in.

Sessions can be listed and ended individually. Changing a password ends every other session, and directory sync on Foundry removes access within minutes of an account being disabled upstream.

Reporting a weakness

Send anything you find to security@kettleforge.example. We acknowledge a report within one working day, tell you what we have reproduced within five, and keep you informed until it is fixed. We will credit you when we publish the fix unless you ask us not to. We do not run a paid bounty, and we will not pursue anyone who reports in good faith, stays within their own organisation's data, and gives us time to fix the problem before describing it publicly.

Automated scanning of the platform is fine against an organisation you own. Denial of service testing, social engineering of our staff, and anything that touches another customer's data are not.
