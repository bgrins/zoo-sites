Sending stock to the studio

The studio proofs on your paper, not on ours. What arrives in the yard therefore decides how quickly the order gets onto a press, and a badly packed parcel costs more days than a badly written brief.

What to send

  1. Enough stock for three pulls plus twenty spare sheets from the same making. Sheets from two different makings are two different substrates as far as the press is concerned, however identical the label.
  2. The reference you want matched: an approved sheet from an earlier run, a printed sample of the finished article, or the library number the librarian gave you. Send the thing itself rather than a photograph of it.
  3. The press and profile pairing your job runs under, copied from the quotation. If you do not know it, say what the job is for and the librarian will pair it.
  4. The order number on your quotation, written on the outside of the parcel as well as on the packing note inside.

Packing

Ship flat and rigid, interleaved with clean paper, in a box rather than a bag. Do not fold a reference sheet, do not clip it, and do not write on its face. A crease through the area being matched makes the reference useless, and the studio will stop and ask rather than proof against a damaged sheet. Wrap against damp: the yard is close to the water and a parcel that sits in a wet loading bay overnight arrives cockled.

Digital files

Where the job needs artwork, send it as a print-ready file with fonts embedded, trim and bleed marked, and every element you want held to a named separation. The studio does not redraw artwork and does not repair a file quietly; anything that will not image is sent back with a note the same day. Files go to the bench address below, or on a memory stick in the parcel with the stock.

Delivery and collection

Everything comes in and goes out through the yard gate, which is open from eight until four on weekdays. There is no counter and nobody at the front of the building, so a courier needs the gate instructions rather than the street address. Tell the bench when a parcel is on its way and the studio will book the make-ready before it lands, which usually saves a day. Unused stock is returned with the job or held for sixty days.
