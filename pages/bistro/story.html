Our Story

How the corner was won

The room at 14 Waldron Lane spent thirty years as a printer's stockroom, and the presses left the floor stained a colour we could never lift. We took the lease in 2011 with a second-hand wood oven, eleven chairs and no sign, and for the first month the neighbours had to guess what we were by the smell coming through the side door. The green awning went up that autumn, and the eleven chairs became twenty-eight.

Nothing about the plan has changed since. We cook a short card, we cook it the same day, and we stop when the day's cooking is gone rather than reaching for something frozen to keep the room turning.

The morning market

Two of us walk to the market barrows before six and buy what looks best, not what a printed menu has already promised. That is why the card is short, why it moves, and why the counter can tell you which grower sent the squash. Bread comes from a bakehouse four streets over, cheese from a single dairy we have used since the second year, and the herbs from the yard behind the kitchen when the yard is willing.

If the market has nothing worth carrying home, a dish simply is not on the card that day. We would rather leave a gap than serve a dish we would not eat standing up at the pass.

Everything is made at the pass, to order, by whoever is standing at it.

Built to order

Every dish is built rather than plated from a script. Leave a component off, add something on, take a medium or a large: the counter will read the ticket back to you before the kitchen starts, and the order-ahead counter asks the same questions in the same order. Cooks change, the card changes, and the habit of asking does not.

We keep no service charge and take no booking. Walk in, or send a ticket ahead and collect it at the counter. If you want to know what the day's special is before you leave the house, ring the kitchen on (503) 555-0180 and someone will shout it down the room.
