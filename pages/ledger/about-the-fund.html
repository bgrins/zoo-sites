What the fund pays for

The operating fund carries the day-to-day cost of holding land in trust: fuel and mileage for the survey truck, culverts and gate furniture, the seed used on restoration ground, the software the office runs on, and the filing fees that come with every permit. It does not carry the cost of acquiring land. Purchases of ground, and the legal work attached to them, are posted to the acquisition fund and appear in a separate set of folios kept by the trust's solicitors.

Restricted gifts are also kept out. Where a donor gives for a named purpose, the gift and the spending against it are held in a restricted fund of its own so that the restriction stays visible for as long as it applies. Money moves between the restricted funds and the operating fund only on a trustee resolution, and the resolution number is written into the description of the posting that carries it.

How a posting reaches the ledger

Every posting begins with a document: an invoice, a receipt, a mileage claim signed by the person who drove, or a bank statement line where no other document exists. The bookkeeper enters the posting on the day the document is settled rather than the day it was issued, which is why the ledger runs on a cash basis and why a January invoice can sit in a February folio. The document is then filed under the same date, so any posting in the ledger can be traced to a piece of paper in the office within a few minutes.

Descriptions are written by hand and are meant to be read by a trustee, not parsed by a machine. The same expense recurring through the year will usually carry the same description word for word, and that repetition is deliberate: it makes a run of related spending legible down a folio without anyone having to sort it.

Review and publication

Folios are published as they are completed and are marked unaudited until the annual review is finished. Harrow & Pike examine the fund once a year against the filed documents and report to the trustees; where the review moves a posting, the original entry stays in place and a correcting entry is made on the date the correction was agreed. Nothing is deleted from a published folio, and no figure in a published folio is edited after the fact.

The trust publishes the ledger because the ground it holds was given by the people who live around it. A neighbour who wants to know what the trust spent on the north parcel should be able to find out without writing a letter first.
