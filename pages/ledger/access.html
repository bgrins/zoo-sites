What is published and what is not

Every posting against the operating fund is published, with its date, its description, its tag and its amount. What is not published is the paperwork behind it. An invoice carries a supplier's bank details and a mileage claim carries a volunteer's home address, so the documents stay in the office and are shown to the trustees, to the auditors, and to anyone with a lawful right to see them.

Names of individuals are kept out of descriptions as a matter of practice. Where a description would otherwise name someone, it names the work instead. A trustee who wants to know who was paid can ask the bookkeeper, who will answer from the filed document.

How long the records are kept

Published folios are kept indefinitely. A folio that has been published is never withdrawn, and a correction is made as a fresh posting rather than by editing what is already on the sheet. Documents supporting the postings are kept for seven full fiscal years after the year they belong to, then destroyed under the trustees' retention schedule; a request that touches a year older than that may find the folio still standing and the paperwork gone.

Earlier years are held as bound sheets in the office rather than on this site. The office will copy a folio from a closed year on request, at the cost of copying, and will send it by post or by email as you prefer.

Reusing the figures

The trust is glad to see the ledger used by town conservation commissions, students and journalists, and asks three things of anyone who republishes it.

  • Attribute the figures to Trelowen Land Trust and say which fiscal year they cover.
  • Say whether the folios you used were marked unaudited when you took them, since the annual review can add correcting entries afterwards.
  • Do not present a figure of your own as the trust's, and do not describe an extract as the complete ledger unless it is one.

Automated collection is acceptable at a gentle rate. If you need the whole year, take the CSV export from any folio once rather than fetching every sheet repeatedly; it is the same data in a form that is easier for you and lighter for us.

Corrections and complaints

If you believe a posting is wrong, write to the bookkeeper with the folio number and the date and say what you think the error is. The office answers within ten business days. Where the bookkeeper agrees, a correcting entry is made on the date the correction is agreed and the original entry stays where it is, so the record of the mistake survives alongside its remedy. Where the bookkeeper does not agree, you may put the question to the trustees, who consider bookkeeping complaints at their quarterly meeting.
