Sizing, fit and care

Measure over a base layer. Our cut runs true, with room for a midlayer.

  • How we cut

    Every shell and midlayer is graded from a single block, so a size that fits you in a fleece fits you in a hardshell with a fleece underneath. Waterproof outers carry roughly four centimetres more room through the chest than the equivalent midlayer, which is deliberate: they are meant to go over the top, not next to the skin. Nothing in the range is cut athletic or relaxed as a style choice.

  • Taking your measurements

    Chest, round the fullest part with the tape level and your arms down. Waist, at the natural crease rather than at the trouser band. Sleeve, from the bone at the back of the neck, over a bent elbow, to the wrist. Take each one twice and use the larger figure. If two of the three put you in different sizes, follow the chest for jackets and the waist for trousers.

  • Between two sizes

    Go up for shells and quilts, and stay down for fleeces and base layers. A hardshell that is snug over a midlayer will pull at the shoulder every time you reach, and no amount of wear will fix it. A fleece that is a touch close will settle within a week. Boot and glove sizes do not work this way; go with the measured size and let the counter check it.

  • Boots and the sock you will wear

    Bring the sock you actually walk in and try boots in the afternoon, when your foot has spread. There should be a thumb's width in front of the longest toe with the heel pushed back, and no lift at the heel on a slope. Estuary and wader patterns are cut wide enough for a thick winter sock, so they will feel roomy over a summer one. Duncrieff keeps a fitting ramp and takes appointments.

  • Washing and re-proofing

    Wash waterproofs on a warm cycle with a technical detergent, never with softener, and tumble them warm for twenty minutes to bring the finish back. Down goes in on a cold cycle with a down wash and dries slowly with a couple of clean tennis balls in the drum. When water stops beading on a shell, the fabric is not finished; it needs re-proofing.

  • Repairs before replacement

    A failed zip, a pulled seam or a snapped pole section is a repair, not the end of a garment. The Quay Lane workshop handles all three, and our re-proofing service will get another few seasons out of a shell that has stopped shedding rain.

Not right? Returns are free for sixty days, worn or unworn, provided the kit is clean.
