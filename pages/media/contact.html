In an emergency

A vessel in distress should not telephone. Call on the announce channel, or use the distress alerting on your set, and the rescue coordination centre is brought in by the operator on watch.

The duty desk relays and records; it does not coordinate a rescue itself. Give the station name you are calling, your vessel name, your position and the nature of the problem, then stand by on the channel you called on rather than searching for another.

Duty desk

Telephone 0117 496 0261 or write to duty@skerrowcoastal.example. The desk is staffed continuously by the operator not on the microphone, so a call during a scheduled transmission is answered as soon as the bulletin ends.

Reception faultAsk for a test transmission on a working channel.

Purged recordingGive the transmission time and the desk reads the forecast from the log.

Hazard sightingAn obstruction, a light out, a container adrift. Position and time, as closely as you have them.

Standing noticeA survey or a works period you want carried in the bulletin.

Station office

Telephone 0117 496 0248 on weekdays between 0900 and 1700 local time, or write to the Harbour Station address. The office handles licensing correspondence, records requests, transcript and log copies beyond the retention window, invitations to speak to a skippers' association, and complaints about anything the station did or failed to do.

Put a records request in writing with the transmission dates and times you need, because the log is searched by time rather than by subject. Requests covering more than a week of transmissions take longer, and the office will tell you how long when it acknowledges.

Corrections

If something read on air was wrong, tell the duty desk at once rather than the office. A correction goes out at the next scheduled transmission, and immediately on the working channel where the error concerned a hazard to navigation. The published transcript is annotated rather than quietly rewritten, so the record shows both what was said and what replaced it.

Finding us

Harbour Station is the stone building at the head of the east pier, reached on foot from the Cape Ardnoy Road turning by the fish market. There is no visitor parking on the pier itself. The station is an operational site, so call the office before coming; the operations room cannot admit callers who arrive unannounced during a watch.
