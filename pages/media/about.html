About the station

Who keeps the watch, under what licence, and how the forecast is put together.

What we are

Skerrow Coastal Radio is a marine safety broadcast service working the northern approaches from Cape Ardnoy to the Fetlan bank. The station transmits the coastal forecast four times a day, repeats gale warnings on receipt, keeps a continuous listening watch on the announce channel, and relays traffic between vessels and the rescue coordination centre when asked.

We are not a broadcaster in the entertainment sense and carry no advertising. The transmitter exists to put weather and navigational information in front of people at sea who cannot otherwise get it.

Harbour Station

The station occupies the stone building at the head of the east pier on Cape Ardnoy Road, which began life as a pilot house. The operations room looks north over the entrance, which matters more than it sounds: the operator on watch can see the sea state being described in the inshore statement.

The aerial sits on the ridge behind the building rather than on the roof, on a mast shared with the harbour authority. A standby generator carries the transmitter and the recorder, and is exercised under load every week.

How a bulletin is assembled

The duty operator takes the synopsis and the sea area forecast from the meteorological service on the wire, then adds what only Skerrow holds: hourly returns from the four reporting stations, the state of any standing notice, and the inshore statement written from the returns and from observation. Nothing is read on air that has not been checked against a second source in the log.

Every transmission is recorded, timed and logged. The recording is published to the bulletin list with a transcript, and the paper log is retained far longer than the audio; that log is what the duty desk reads from when a mariner asks for a forecast whose recording has already been purged.

Licence and oversight

Forecast material is reproduced under marine safety licence MS-114, which sets the transmission schedule, the retention period for recordings and the standard the station is held to for accuracy and availability. An outage affecting a scheduled transmission is reportable, and is published as a standing notice on the bulletin list.

Operators hold a general operator certificate and are re-assessed annually on distress procedure. Two are on the roster at all times, one on the microphone and one on the watch.

Working with us

Harbour Station is an operational site and is not open to visitors without an appointment. The operations room cannot take general enquiries on the announce channel.

Skippers' associations, harbour masters and survey operators who want a standing notice carried should write rather than call, so that the wording read on air is the wording they intended. The contact page sets out where each kind of enquiry should go.
