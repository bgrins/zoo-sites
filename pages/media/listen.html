How to listen

Set the receiver once and the coastal forecast finds you four times a day.

The announce call

Every transmission opens with a short announce call on channel 16, naming the station and the working channel that will carry the bulletin. Keep a watch on 16, then shift to 26 or 62 when the call names it. The bulletin never runs on 16 itself, because the announce channel must stay clear for distress traffic.

The working channel moves with traffic on the approaches and with survey work in the sound, so listen for the channel named rather than assuming last night's.

Setting up a receiver

A fixed set with a masthead whip will hear Skerrow anywhere inside the coverage described on the sea areas page. A handheld held at the chart table often will not, and the difference is almost always the aerial rather than the set. Three things are worth checking before you call the duty desk about reception.

SquelchWind it back until you hear noise, then forward until the noise just stops.

Dual watchLeave it on for 16 and one working channel; turn it off while the bulletin runs.

AerialLook for a green cable end or a cracked base insulator before suspecting the radio.

Reading along

Each transmission is recorded off air and published to the bulletin list within a few minutes, with a written transcript alongside it and chapter marks for the synopsis, the sea areas, the station reports and the inshore statement. A chapter mark is the quickest way to reach one sea area without listening through the whole forecast.

Recordings are held for six hours from transmission and then purged. The written log is kept far longer, and the duty desk will read or send any earlier forecast from it, quoting the transmission time.

If you cannot hear us

Do not wait for the next scheduled bulletin to find out whether the fault is yours or ours. Call the duty desk on 0117 496 0261 and ask for a test transmission on a working channel.

Skerrow works the northern approaches from a single site at Harbour Station, so a vessel in the lee of high ground on the Orrin shore can sit in a shadow that clears within a mile. Where a shadow is persistent and charted, the duty desk will relay the forecast to you by voice on the working channel on request.
