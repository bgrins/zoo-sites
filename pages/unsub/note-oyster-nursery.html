Night Shift at the Oyster Nursery

Filed by Tobiah Lusk · August 31, 2026

Seed oysters are counted at low tide, and this week low tide fell at a little after one in the morning. So that is when the crew at the Salt Pond nursery came down to the upwellers with headlamps, clipboards and a thermos of tea.

The seed at this stage is about the size of a fingernail. It lives in mesh-bottomed bins through which pumped seawater flows upward, carrying food past the young oysters. Every few weeks the bins are pulled, the seed is sieved into size classes, and a sample from each class is counted by hand to estimate how many are growing and how many have died.

We were given a sample tray and a counter and asked to help. We lost count twice. The crew was kind about it. One of them, who has done this for six seasons, said everyone loses count the first night, and that the trick is to count in fives and never look up.

The survival numbers were good this year: better than eighty percent in the larger size class. The seed moves out to the floating cages on the pond next month.
