Technique: A Sharper Knife in Four Minutes

By Ruth Abernall, kitchen editor · September 3, 2026

Most dull knives are not dull. The edge has rolled over to one side, like the tip of a bent nail, and it only needs pushing back into line. That is what a honing rod does, and it takes less time than finding a clean towel.

The motion

Hold the rod upright with its tip resting on a folded towel on the counter. Set the heel of the blade against the top of the rod at about fifteen degrees, roughly the angle you get by halving a right angle and halving it again. Draw the knife down and toward you so the whole edge, heel to tip, crosses the rod in one smooth stroke. Repeat on the other side. Six strokes a side is plenty.

How often

Hone every time you take the knife out for a real job, not once a month. A rolled edge that is caught early goes back with a few light strokes; one that has been used for weeks has started to wear away and needs a stone.

When honing is not enough

If a knife still skids on a tomato skin after honing, it needs sharpening, which removes metal to make a new edge. Once or twice a year is typical for a home cook. Our workshop partners sharpen members' knives at every supper club; bring them wrapped in a towel.
