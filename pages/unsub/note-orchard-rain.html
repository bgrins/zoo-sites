An Orchard Waits for Rain

Filed by Maren Hask · September 8, 2026

At Hollis Hill Orchard three generations are watching the same patch of sky over the ridge. It has rained twice since the middle of July, both times for less than an hour.

The apples are small this year, and the family is not unhappy about it. A dry late summer concentrates sugar, and the fruit that has come in so far, early Macs and a few rows of an old russet nobody else seems to grow anymore, is intensely sweet. For eating apples, that is a mixed blessing. For cider, it may be a very good year.

Walt Hollis, who planted the oldest trees, remembers a dry year in the 1990s that made the best cider the farm ever pressed. His granddaughter, who runs the press now, keeps a notebook of sugar readings from every pressing since she took over. She would not commit to a comparison. She did let us taste this week's juice, which was thick, bright, and nearly as sweet as syrup.

If the rain holds off another two weeks, the first cider will be ready at the end of October.
