The Diner at the End of Route 9

By Ines Carrow · August 27, 2026

The Endcap seats eleven people: seven at the counter and four in the single booth by the window, which has a view of the turnaround where Route 9 gives up and becomes a gravel lot. It opens at 5:30 in the morning and closes when the pie runs out, which on a summer Saturday can be before noon.

Deb Marchetti has owned it for twenty-two years. She makes three pies a day, by hand, and she will not make a fourth. "If I make four, people expect four," she says. "Then I'm a pie factory with a grill."

The regulars

Most of the counter belongs to people who have eaten breakfast there for a decade or more. There is a lobsterman who orders two eggs over easy and a slice of whatever pie is left from yesterday. There is a retired schoolteacher who does the crossword and asks the counter for help only with the sports clues. There is a highway crew that arrives at 6:15 every weekday in the warm months and fills every seat.

The menu fits on a single laminated card. Nothing on it has changed price since 2019, and Deb says nothing will until the price of butter makes her.

Closing time

On the morning we visited, the last slice of blueberry went at 11:40. Deb wiped the counter, flipped the sign, and sat down in the booth with a cup of coffee. She was back at 4:45 the next morning, rolling out crust.
