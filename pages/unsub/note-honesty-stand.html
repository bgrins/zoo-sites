The Honesty Stand Ledger

Filed by Ines Carrow · August 24, 2026

The farm stand on the Shore Road has no cashier. It has a wooden shelf of whatever the Abernathy family picked that morning, a price list written in marker on a board, and a coffee tin with a slot cut in the lid.

It also has a ledger. Every evening for fourteen years, Carol Abernathy has counted the tin and written two numbers in a spiral notebook: what the produce on the shelf should have brought in, and what the tin actually holds. The notebooks fill a shelf in her kitchen.

The two numbers almost always agree. Most days the tin is a dollar or two over, which Carol attributes to people who do not want to wait for change. A few times a summer it is short, usually by the price of a single item. Twice in fourteen years it was empty, and both times, she says, an envelope of cash turned up in the tin within the week with a note saying sorry.

She has never considered putting in a camera. "The ledger tells me everything I need to know," she says. "People are mostly good at arithmetic when nobody is watching."
