The house

Granville Street · Playing since 1908

A working single-stage playhouse, restored twice, closed once, and still lit by the same brass switchboard in the prompt corner.

Built for a variety bill

The Aurelia opened in 1908 as a variety house at the quiet end of Granville Street, put up in fourteen months by a syndicate of local drapers who wanted somewhere to spend their evenings. The architect gave them a raked stalls floor, a horseshoe circle on cast-iron columns, and a stage house tall enough to fly a full cloth. Everything about the room was arranged for a bill of short turns: a shallow apron, an orchestra rail rather than a pit, and a centre aisle running straight from the foyer doors to the stage so that latecomers could be seated between numbers.

That aisle is still there, and it still shapes how the house sells. The stalls break into a left block and a right block on either side of it, and no pair of seats spans the gap, which is the first thing the counter explains to anyone booking for two.

Dark years and the second restoration

The playhouse went dark in 1961, spent eleven years as a furniture warehouse, and lost its plasterwork ceiling roses to a leaking roof in that time. A trust bought the shell in 1972 and reopened the stalls floor two seasons later with the circle boarded off. The second restoration, the one visitors see now, rebuilt the foyer, put the gilding back on the proscenium and returned the house to eight rows of stalls, A nearest the stage.

The circle is closed again this season while its plasterwork is made good. Its supporting columns break the sight line from a handful of seats at the outer ends of some stalls rows, and we sell those as restricted view at the counter rather than pretending the view is whole.

The company today

Aurelia Theatres Ltd runs the building with a permanent staff of nine and a house band that has played here since the trust's first season. We produce two shows a year of our own and let the stage the rest of the time, which is how a house this size stays open.
