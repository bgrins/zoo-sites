Privacy notice

What the mutual records about you, why it records it, and what it will never do with it.

What we collect

Two kinds of information reach us. The first you give us: the answers on Form HQ-1, the address of the dwelling, the name and telephone number of the applicant, and whatever you tell an adjuster when you report a loss. The second we gather to rate or to settle: the exterior inspection report, loss history from the industry claims exchange, and public records of the property. We do not obtain credit reports for rating, and we do not buy consumer behaviour data from brokers.

Why we hold it

  • To rate an application and to issue, endorse or renew a policy.
  • To investigate, adjust and pay a claim, and to pursue recovery from a party at fault.
  • To meet the reporting duties a state insurance department places on a licensed carrier.
  • To detect and to prove fraud, which raises the premium of every honest policyholder.

Who sees it

Inside the mutual, access follows the file: the underwriter who rated it, the adjuster carrying it, the policyholder advocate on a review, and the audit staff. Outside the mutual, information goes to the inspection contractor, the industry claims exchange, reinsurers who share the risk, counsel where a loss is litigated, and a regulator that requires it.

We do not sell policyholder information, and we do not rent our policyholder list for marketing by anyone, including affiliates.

Your rights

Write to the Office of the Policyholder Advocate at 400 Assay Court, Harmon Falls, or to policyholders@cresthaven-mutual.example, to see the personal information we hold about you, to have an error corrected, or to record your disagreement with something we decline to change. A copy of your file is provided within fifteen business days at no charge.

Where we corrected a record, we tell anyone we had already disclosed the incorrect version to. Where we declined, your statement is filed with the record so that anyone reading it reads both.

Retention and this website

An underwriting file is kept for seven years after a policy ends; a claim file for seven years after final payment; a declined application for three years. Then they are destroyed.

The quotation desk keeps a working record of an application in progress so that you can leave the form and come back to it. It holds only the answers you have given, it carries no advertising or analytics tags, and it is discarded when the quotation expires thirty days after issue.

A telephone call to the policyholder line may be recorded for quality and training. You will be told at the start of the call, and you may ask for the recording to be stopped without any effect on the service you receive.
