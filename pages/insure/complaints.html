Complaints and appeals

Three stages, each with a stated clock, and an appeal that does not go back to the person who decided.

1

Raise it with the person handling your file

Telephone your adjuster, or the underwriter who signed the letter you are unhappy with. Most complaints are a gap in the record rather than a difference of view, and the person holding the file can usually close the gap the same day.

Ask for the complaint to be logged even where it is resolved on the call. A logged complaint is counted in the department's quarterly report to the board; an unlogged one teaches the mutual nothing.

2

Ask for review by the policyholder advocate

Write to the Office of the Policyholder Advocate at 400 Assay Court, Harmon Falls, NE 69517, or by electronic mail to policyholders@cresthaven-mutual.example. The advocate reports to the board, not to claims or underwriting, and reviews the file independently of whoever decided it.

State the outcome you are seeking. An appeal that says only that the decision was wrong takes longer than one that says what would put it right.

3

Appraisal or appeal to the committee

Where the dispute is the amount of a loss rather than whether it is covered, your policy provides for appraisal: each side names an appraiser, and the two name an umpire. Where the dispute is coverage, the appeals committee of the board hears it at its monthly sitting, and you may appear or send a representative.

Neither route waives any right you hold. You may complain to your state insurance department at any stage, and doing so does not close your file with us.

The clock we keep

Acknowledgement
Two business days from receipt, in writing.
Stage one answer
Ten business days.
Advocate review
Thirty calendar days, extended once with reasons.
Committee sitting
Monthly; papers close ten days before.
File copy on request
Fifteen business days, at no charge.
A complaint never suspends cover. Premium remains due while a complaint is open, and a policy is not canceled or non-renewed because a policyholder complained. If you believe a renewal decision followed a complaint, say so to the advocate and the review is escalated to stage three at once.
