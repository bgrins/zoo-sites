Brass-cased two-day marine chronometer

Halloway and Sons, Portsmouth, circa 1904

This lot is under the hammer now. Bidding is live in the room as you read this, and once the hammer falls the sale is final. If you mean to bid, have your figure ready before the auctioneer gives fair warning.

The three-tier rosewood case with brass corner straps and a sunk brass handle to each side, the lid inset with an ivorine plate engraved for the maker and numbered to the movement. The gimballed bowl retains its locking screw.

Silvered dial signed Halloway and Sons, Makers to the Admiralty, Portsmouth, with subsidiary seconds and a state-of-wind sector reading to fifty-six hours. Blued steel hands. Spotted plates, Earnshaw-type spring detent escapement, Harrison's maintaining power, and a cut bimetallic balance with cylindrical weights.

Condition report

The movement runs and sets when wound and appears to have been serviced in the recent past, although no paperwork accompanies the service. The dial is clean with one small scratch below the seconds subsidiary. The case has shrinkage to the lid and a replacement escutcheon; the outer tier retains its glazed panel. The gimbal ring is free and the locking screw engages. Sold as found and not guaranteed as a timekeeper.

Provenance

Acquired from the estate of a Wiltshire collector, 1987, and thence by descent. Exhibited at the Cheverell Wharf horological weekend, 2004.
