SP-12 / STAGE CLEARANCE
Standing procedure for keypad clearance at the facility access console. Issued by site engineering, reviewed each quarter. Supersedes SP-11.

Scope

This sheet covers clearance entry on the keypad module at FAC-CONSOLE / 07 and what happens when an entry is rejected. It does not cover door release from the plant room panel, which is held under the mechanical isolation procedure, nor visitor escort, which stays with the operations desk.

Before you key anything

  1. Confirm the console is yours to work. Only the operator named on the shift sheet keys a clearance. A second operator observing is welcome; a second operator keying is not.
  2. Read the lamp strip. The interlock lamp must be lit and no door lamp may show held. A held door means the loading crew still has a leaf open, and the stage will refuse to clear until it closes.
  3. Read the event log. The last self-test entry should be from the current shift. An older one means the module has not been exercised since rotation, and site engineering wants to know before you key a code, not after.

Codes and rotation

Clearance codes are issued by the operations desk at each shift rotation and are valid for that shift only. Codes travel by voice or on the paper shift sheet. Nobody writes one on the console, on the frame, in the log, or in a message; anything written down goes in the shredder at handover.

Ask the desk to reissue rather than reusing yesterday's. A code that has rolled off is indistinguishable at the keypad from one that was never valid, and it counts as a rejection just the same.

Keying a clearance

Key the code exactly as it was issued, including dashes. The keypad does not correct spacing and does not accept a partial entry. Press Unlock once and wait for the module to answer; the status line under the keypad carries the result. Do not press Unlock a second time while the line reads that a check is in progress, because the module treats the second press as a fresh attempt.

Rejections

A rejected code leaves the interlock closed and writes a line to the event log with the attempt count for the shift. The third rejection in a shift pages the duty engineer, who reads the log, records the reason on the shift sheet and rings the console. Before keying again after a rejection, check the code with the operations desk rather than trying variations at the keypad.

Recording Every rejected attempt writes an entry to the console event log with the time, and every granted clearance is written to the site access record against the operator's shift line; the stage lamp turns green while it is in force. The log is pulled weekly into the site access record and held for two years. If you keyed a clearance on someone else's behalf, say so on the shift sheet so the two records agree.
