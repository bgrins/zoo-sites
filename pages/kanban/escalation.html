When to escalate

Escalate as soon as a decision is above your authority, not once the delay has already happened. Four situations always escalate: a fault that will hold a vessel past its departure time, plant that cannot be isolated safely, a crew request that would leave a berth without cover, and a work order nobody will accept.

What to have ready

Give the work order reference, the berth or gate, the tag it was raised with, and what you have already tried. If the query is about the board, give the board revision from the last save; without it the duty controller cannot tell which layout you are describing. Say plainly whether the board has been saved since the change you are asking about.

Callout

A callout brings a fitter or electrician onto the terminal outside their roster. It is arranged by the duty controller and needs the berth supervisor's approval when it is raised against a single work order. Shared crews lent to another terminal are recalled for urgent work only, at thirty minutes' notice, and the terminal they are leaving is told before the crew is.

After the shift

Every escalation goes in the handover entry, with the time and the name of the person you reached. An escalation that produced no answer is still recorded; the incoming supervisor needs to know a call is outstanding.
