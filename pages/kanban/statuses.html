Status reference

The words this console uses, and what each one commits somebody to. Terminal 3 uses the same vocabulary as Terminals 1 and 2; local variations are not permitted.

Work order tags

Urgent Cannot wait for the next planned window
Set by the person raising the order when the fault stops a vessel working, threatens a departure time, or puts a crew at risk. An urgent tag is a claim on a fitter now, so the raiser names themselves on it.
Blocked Work cannot start for a reason outside the crew
A part on order, a permit not yet issued, a contractor not yet on site, or plant that cannot be isolated until a berth clears. The tag is removed by whoever clears the obstruction, not by the crew waiting on it.
Routine Real work, no time pressure
Wear, cosmetic damage, and anything that can wait for a planned window. Routine orders are worked in raised order unless a supervisor says otherwise.

Berth states

Occupied A vessel is alongside
The register carries the vessel, the cargo and the expected departure. Work on quay plant serving an occupied berth needs the berth supervisor's agreement.
Vacant No vessel, and available unless held
A vacant berth may still be held, for a survey or for an inbound allocation. A held berth is not free for reallocation until the hold is closed.
Closed Withdrawn from service
Structural work, a failed inspection, or plant out of use. A closed berth carries a reopening date, and the date moves only with the harbour master's agreement.

Crew states

On quay Available on the terminal, on radio
Reachable on the crew's radio channel and able to take work without a callout.
On water Afloat on the launch
Reachable, but not available for quay work until the launch is back alongside.
Terminal 2 Lent to a neighbouring terminal
Recalled through the duty controller at thirty minutes' notice. Shared crews are recalled for urgent work only.
Off site Leave, training or sickness
Not to be called. The roster carries the expected return; the duty controller arranges cover.

Handover entries

A handover entry is either signed off or open. Signed off means the outgoing supervisor has read the saved board and stands behind the summary. An open entry is a shift the incoming supervisor has queried, and it stays open until both supervisors agree the correction.

Changing the vocabulary

These words are set in the terminal operating instructions and are not local to the console. Requests to add a tag or a state go to the dispatch systems group with the harbour master's endorsement, and take effect at a quarter boundary so every terminal changes on the same day.
