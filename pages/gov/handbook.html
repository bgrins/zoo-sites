Section 1 — Purpose & Authority

This handbook is issued under the authority delegated to the Director by the bureau charter and consolidates requirements previously scattered across divisional memoranda, some of which date to the paper-ledger era.

Where a divisional instruction conflicts with this handbook, the handbook controls unless the Director has issued a written waiver naming the provision waived and its expiry date. Waivers are logged by the Office of Compliance Counsel and lapse automatically if not renewed.

Nothing in this handbook enlarges the bureau's statutory authority. A provision found to exceed that authority is severable, and the remainder of the handbook continues in force.

Section 2 — Definitions

Capitalized terms used in this handbook carry the meanings assigned in this section; a term not defined here takes its ordinary administrative meaning.

A covered action is any determination, filing disposition, payment application, or records disposition that affects the account of an identifiable filer. A business day excludes weekends, bureau holidays, and the monthly records-processing closure. The system of record for a covered action is the system designated in the responsible division's desk procedure.

The glossary in the appendix restates these definitions alphabetically for convenience; where the appendix and this section differ, this section governs.

Section 3 — Scope of Application

The provisions of this handbook apply to all bureau personnel, to designated contractors while performing bureau work, and to interns and detailees for the duration of their assignment.

Obligations arising under an applicable interagency agreement are unaffected; where an agreement imposes a stricter requirement than this handbook, the stricter requirement is observed.

Elected officials and employees of other Commonwealth agencies are outside the scope of this handbook except while exercising authority delegated by the Director in writing.

Section 4 — Roles & Responsibilities

The Director bears ultimate responsibility for compliance and appoints the Compliance Committee, which consists of the deputy director, one chief from each division, and the Compliance Counsel as a non-voting adviser.

Division chiefs implement this handbook through desk procedures tailored to their systems and staffing, and certify annually that those procedures remain consistent with the current revision.

Unit supervisors verify quarterly that day-to-day practice within their units matches the applicable desk procedure and report the results to the Committee, which may direct corrective measures, additional training, or a targeted audit.

Section 5 — Registration Requirements

Personnel who open, key, or dispose of declarations must hold a current processing registration issued by the Personnel Office.

Registrations are renewed each January. A lapsed registration suspends the holder's system access until renewal is complete; work performed during a lapse must be reviewed and countersigned by a registered colleague.

The Personnel Office publishes the registration roster to division chiefs monthly. Discrepancies between the roster and actual duty assignments are reportable events under Section 8.

Back to top

Section 6 — Licensing Standards

Field assessors and hearing officers must hold the professional license prescribed for their role and must complete the continuing education hours the licensing body requires.

Proof of license renewal is filed with the Personnel Office within ten business days of issuance. An employee whose license is suspended or restricted must notify their division chief before the start of the next duty day.

Work requiring a license that was performed while the license was invalid is voidable and must be re-performed or ratified by a licensed reviewer before it may support a covered action.

Section 7 — Permit Correspondence

All correspondence concerning a permit application is assigned the permit file number on receipt and logged in the permit register, whether it arrives by mail, by counter delivery, or by facsimile.

Outbound notices of deficiency and final determinations are issued over the signature of the responsible section head and copied to the permit file the same day they are mailed.

Undeliverable correspondence is annotated with the returned envelope and a note of any address research performed; it is not destroyed merely because delivery failed.

Retention requirement. Permit correspondence, including applications, notices of deficiency, and final determinations, must be retained for 3 years from the date the permit file is closed, after which it is transferred to the records center for disposition.

Section 8 — Reporting Obligations

Each division submits a monthly exception report to the Compliance Committee listing covered actions completed outside the documentation window, registration lapses, and unresolved reconciliation items.

A reportable event — a suspected loss of filer data, an unauthorized disposition of records, or an offer of a prohibited gratuity — is reported to the Compliance Counsel within two business days of discovery, in writing, regardless of whether the event was remedied.

Nil reports are required; the absence of a report is treated as a missing report, not as an absence of exceptions.

Section 9 — Disclosure Requirements

Employees in designated positions file an annual statement of financial interests with the Office of Compliance Counsel by March 31, and an amended statement within thirty days of any material change.

The list of designated positions is set by the Committee and reviewed when duties change; an employee moved into a designated position files an initial statement before assuming the duties that make the position designated.

Statements are confidential personnel records. Access is limited to the Compliance Counsel, the Director, and, on written request, the filer.

Section 10 — Conflicts of Interest

An employee may not take a covered action affecting their own account, the account of a household member, or the account of a business in which they hold an interest.

Recusal is made in writing to the unit supervisor before the action is assigned or, where the conflict emerges mid-review, as soon as it is recognized. The supervisor reassigns the matter and notes the recusal in the file.

Doubtful cases are resolved by the Compliance Counsel; pending that determination the employee treats the conflict as real and stands aside.

Section 11 — Gifts & Gratuities

Personnel may not accept any gift, favor, or thing of value from a filer, a representative of a filer, or a vendor seeking bureau business.

The only exceptions are unsolicited advertising items of nominal value and refreshments of negligible value offered in the ordinary course of a meeting. When in doubt, the item is declined or surrendered to the unit supervisor for return.

An offer that appears intended to influence a covered action is a reportable event under Section 8 whether or not the item was accepted.

Section 12 — Procurement Conduct

From the issuance of a solicitation until award, personnel communicate with bidders only through the Purchasing Office, and every substantive question is answered by numbered addendum distributed to all holders of the bid packet.

Evaluation worksheets are completed independently by each evaluator before panel discussion and are surrendered to the Purchasing Office with the evaluation record.

An employee who has had business or personal dealings with a bidder within the preceding two years discloses the relationship to the Purchasing Office before accepting an evaluation assignment.

Section 13 — Information Security

System credentials are issued to named individuals and may not be shared, posted, or embedded in unattended scripts. A terminal left unattended is locked, without exception for brief absences.

Suspected credential compromise is reported to the information security officer before the end of the duty day on which it is suspected, and the credential is disabled pending review.

Removable media may be connected to bureau systems only when issued and inventoried by the information security officer for a stated purpose.

Section 14 — Visitor & Facility Access

Visitors to non-public areas sign in at the guard desk, wear a numbered visitor badge, and remain under escort by the employee they are visiting for the duration of the visit.

Badges are surrendered at sign-out. A badge not returned by close of business is reported to the facilities office, which voids it and notes the discrepancy in the access log.

Access to the records center and to the cashier's cage is further restricted to the roster the responsible section head files with the guard desk each quarter.

Retention requirement. Visitor sign-in sheets and access badge logs must be retained for 18 months from the date of the visit and are then destroyed under the standard disposition schedule, absent a litigation hold.

Section 15 — Data Classification

Bureau information is classified as public, internal, or restricted. Filer account data is restricted by default and is reclassified only by written determination of the Records and Disclosure Division.

Documents are marked with their classification at creation; an unmarked document is handled as internal until classified. Reclassification is noted on the document and in the system of record.

Restricted material may leave bureau premises only in a locked container, under a transmittal record, and by a carrier the Records and Disclosure Division has approved.

Back to top

Section 16 — Public Communications

Forms, instructions, bulletins, and pages published on the bureau site are cleared by the Office of Compliance Counsel for legal sufficiency and by the responsible division for accuracy before release.

Figures quoted in a publication are drawn from the current fee schedule or the current form revision, never restated from memory or from a superseded document.

Corrections to a published document are made by numbered erratum or by a revised edition, not by silent substitution.

Section 17 — Media Inquiries

Inquiries from the press are referred to the public information officer without comment, including requests characterized as informal or off the record.

Personnel do not confirm or deny the existence of any filer's account or of any open matter. Aggregate statistics may be released only from tabulations the Records and Disclosure Division has approved for publication.

A record of each referred inquiry — outlet, caller, subject, and date — is sent to the public information officer the same day.

Back to top

Section 18 — Complaint Handling

Complaints from the public are entered in the complaint register on receipt and acknowledged in writing within ten business days.

A complaint alleging misconduct by bureau personnel is routed unopened to the Compliance Counsel; a complaint concerning the merits of an assessment is routed to the hearings calendar under Section 20 rather than handled as a complaint.

The complainant is informed of the disposition in plain language. Where no action is taken, the reply states why.

Back to top

Section 19 — Investigations

Internal investigations are opened on referral from the Compliance Committee or the Director and are conducted by staff with no reporting relationship to the persons under review.

Once notified of an investigation, personnel may not alter, remove, or dispose of any potentially relevant material; routine disposition schedules are suspended for the matter until the investigator releases the hold.

The existence and subject of an open investigation are disclosed only to those whose duties require the knowledge.

Section 20 — Hearings & Appeals

A filer aggrieved by a covered action is entitled to notice stating the action taken, the basis for it, and the deadline and address for requesting a hearing.

Hearings are presided over by a hearing officer who took no part in the action under review. The record consists of the file, the exhibits admitted, and the recording or transcript of the proceeding.

Decisions issue in writing, state the facts found and the rule applied, and inform the filer of any further appeal available.

Section 21 — Enforcement Actions

Violations of this handbook are addressed under a progressive framework: written counseling, written reprimand, suspension of delegated authority, and revocation of delegated authority.

The step selected reflects the gravity of the violation and any pattern of recurrence; a serious first violation may begin at any step. Enforcement actions are recorded by the Personnel Office and reported in summary to the Committee.

Nothing in this section limits separate action under the personnel rules or referral to an authority outside the bureau where the conduct requires it.

Section 22 — Records & Retention

Compliance with this section is a condition of continued authorization under the bureau charter.

Failure to comply with this section may result in administrative action ranging from written counseling to revocation of delegated authority, consistent with the progressive framework described in the Enforcement Actions section of this handbook.

Questions concerning interpretation of this section should be directed to the Office of Compliance Counsel before the covered action is taken, not after. Informal guidance is advisory; only written determinations bind the bureau.

Each division maintains a desk procedure implementing this section, tailored to its systems and staffing. Desk procedures must be consistent with this handbook and are subject to review during the annual compliance assessment.

The Committee may issue interpretive bulletins clarifying the application of this section to novel circumstances. Bulletins are numbered, indexed in the appendix, and remain in force until incorporated into a subsequent handbook revision.

Retention requirement. Field audit logs must be retained for 7 years from the date of collection. This period reflects the limitations horizon for assessment challenges and applies regardless of the medium in which the logs were captured.

Section 23 — Privacy Safeguards

Filer information is accessed only to the extent a currently assigned duty requires. Browsing an account out of curiosity, including one's own, is a violation regardless of whether any information is disclosed.

Each disclosure of filer information outside the bureau is logged with the recipient, the authority for the disclosure, and the records disclosed.

Documents containing filer information are not left on counters, printers, or unattended desks, and are destroyed by the approved method rather than discarded with ordinary waste.

Back to top

Section 24 — Training & Certification

All personnel complete compliance training annually; new personnel complete it within thirty days of entering on duty and before taking any covered action unsupervised.

Division chiefs certify completion for their divisions each year, and the Personnel Office reconciles the certifications against the training roster.

An employee whose training has lapsed may continue routine duties under supervision but may not sign determinations until the training is brought current.

Back to top

Section 25 — Delegation of Authority

Authority is delegated only by written instrument stating the powers delegated, the position receiving them, and the duration of the delegation.

Sub-delegation requires the Director's prior written approval. A purported delegation made orally, by custom, or by acquiescence has no effect.

The Office of Compliance Counsel maintains the register of delegations, and an action taken outside a registered delegation is voidable under Section 21.

Back to top

Section 26 — Emergency Procedures

Each division maintains a continuity plan naming an alternate approving official and the minimum records needed to continue counter service from an alternate site.

During a declared closure, statutory deadlines administered by the bureau toll as provided by the charter, and the tolling is announced in a numbered bulletin rather than applied case by case.

Drills are conducted annually; deficiencies observed during a drill are treated as findings and tracked to correction.

Back to top

Section 27 — Working Papers & Drafts

Drafts and working papers are marked DRAFT, dated, and kept with the matter file while the matter is open; they are not released outside the bureau and are not cited as authority for a covered action.

A draft that records a determination actually communicated to a filer ceases to be a working paper and is filed as part of the record of that determination.

Retention requirement. Draft assessment worksheets and other preliminary working papers must be retained for 90 days following issuance of the final assessment, after which they are destroyed unless subject to a litigation hold.

Back to top

Section 28 — Vendor Oversight

Every contract under which a vendor handles filer information or bureau records names a contract monitor responsible for compliance oversight of that vendor.

Vendor personnel receive the least access their work requires, complete the same confidentiality acknowledgment as bureau staff, and surrender access on the last day of performance.

The contract monitor reviews vendor performance annually and reports material deficiencies to the Purchasing Office and the Committee.

Back to top

Section 29 — Annual Review Cycle

The Office of Compliance Counsel reviews this handbook each spring. Divisions submit proposed changes by the last business day of February, with the operational reason for each change.

The annual compliance assessment visits each division on a schedule the Committee publishes in January; desk procedures, registers, and logs required by this handbook are examined against practice.

Assessment findings are answered in writing within thirty days and tracked by the Committee until closed.

Back to top

Section 30 — Amendments & Effective Dates

This handbook is amended only by numbered revision issued over the Director's signature; interpretive bulletins may clarify a provision but may not amend it.

A revision takes effect thirty days after issuance unless the revision states another date. Actions begun before the effective date are completed under the provision in force when they began.

Superseded pages are removed from circulating copies and destroyed; the Records and Disclosure Division keeps the archival set of all prior revisions.
