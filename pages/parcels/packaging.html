Packaging guide

Parcels cross several belts and at least two vehicle loads between collection and delivery. Packing to this guide keeps contents intact and keeps compensation cover valid under the terms of carriage.

The box

  • Use a rigid double-walled box for anything over 5 kg or anything fragile.
  • Reused boxes are fine if all flaps are intact and old labels and barcodes are removed or fully covered.
  • Fill the box: contents should not shift when shaken. Use crumpled paper, foam or airbags, not fabric alone.
  • Leave at least 5 cm of cushioning between fragile contents and every wall of the box.

Sealing and labelling

  • Seal every seam with pressure-sensitive tape at least 48 mm wide, in an H pattern on top and bottom.
  • Fix the label flat on the largest face, not across a seam or an edge.
  • Write the receiver's postcode inside the box on a slip of paper; it lets a depot relabel the parcel if the outer label is lost.

Fragile and liquid contents

  • Double-box glass and ceramics: 5 cm of cushioning in the inner box, 5 cm between the boxes.
  • Seal liquids in a leakproof bag inside a lined box; absorbent material must be able to take the full volume.
  • Fragile markings help handlers but do not replace proper cushioning, and cover is assessed on the packing, not the marking.

Charged weight

The charged weight is the greater of the actual weight and the volumetric weight: length x width x height in centimetres, divided by 5,000. A large light box can therefore cost more than a small dense one; see rates and zones for the weight bands.
