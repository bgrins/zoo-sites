Walks from the door

Three marked routes leave the woodshed clearing, and all three come back to it, which matters more on the ridge than the distances do. Paint flashes on the trees carry the colours below. The flashes are repainted each spring and the paths are otherwise unmaintained, so treat every one of them as rougher than its length suggests.

There is no signal on the ridge and no warden. Tell somebody in the valley which route you took, take water, and turn back at the hour you said you would rather than at the point you meant to reach.

The tarn shore, green flashes

Down through the larches to Fernlee tarn and along its northern shore to the jetty, then back by the spring path. An hour at an easy pace, and the only route on the ridge a child manages comfortably. The rowing boat lies at the jetty with a bailer under the thwart; oars live in the woodshed, so carry them down with you. The tarn is deep from the first stroke out and cold in every season, and nobody is watching the water.

The fire tower, red flashes

The long one. From the clearing the path climbs the shoulder behind the cabin, breaks the treeline in a bowl of bilberry, then follows the ridge crest to the old fire tower. Three hours out and back if the ground is dry, four after rain, and the last twenty minutes are hands-on-rock rather than walking. The tower is a shell: the platform is sound, the stair is not, and we ask guests to stay off it. In the right weather you can see the whole tarn and the smoke from your own stove from the bowl below it.

The charcoal round, yellow flashes

A ninety-minute loop through the old coppice on the sheltered side, past four charcoal-burners' platforms cut into the hillside. Nothing stands on them now but the terraces are unmistakable once you have seen the first. The best route in bad weather, since the trees take the worst of the wind and the going is soft underfoot.

What lives up here

  • Red deer on the bowl at first light, gone by mid-morning
  • Goshawk over the coppice, most often in the last hour of daylight
  • Adders on the warm stones by the fire-tower path in high summer
  • Bilberry from midsummer and chanterelle after the first autumn rain

Weather on the ridge

The ridge makes its own conditions. Cloud drops onto the crest with no warning and lifts the same way, wind on the tarn flank runs twice as hard as it does in Fernlee, and a bright morning in the valley is often a wet afternoon up here. Take a shell layer on every walk, however the sky looks from the porch, and keep the stove laid before you leave so there is heat waiting when you come back soaked.
