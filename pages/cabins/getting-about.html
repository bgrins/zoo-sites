Access on the ridge

A hand-built cabin above a forestry track suits some guests and defeats others, and a booking calendar cannot tell you which you are. What follows is an honest description of the ground, the doorways and the fittings, written so you can decide before you reserve rather than discover it at the gate lodge. Ring 01632 960 177 with anything this page does not answer; whoever picks up has carried furniture through every door in the building.

Short version: no step-free route exists from the track to the cabin, the loft is reached by a ladder stair, and the nearest help is thirty minutes down the hill. Nothing here is level and nothing is lit after dark.

From the track to the door

The last approach is forty metres of packed earth path from the parking spur to the porch, rising the height of a storey over that distance, with two timber steps let into the slope where it is steepest. The surface is loose after rain. There is no handrail and no lighting, and a wheelchair or a pushchair will not manage it. The porch itself has a single step up of about twenty centimetres and a raised threshold at the door.

Inside the cabin

Doors and floors

  • Front door clear width just under seventy centimetres
  • Plank floors, level, with a raised sill at every doorway
  • Porch room and box bed both on the entrance floor
  • Low beam over the pantry corner, marked with white paint

Loft and washroom

  • Loft reached by a ladder stair of nine open treads
  • Loft headroom stands full only along the ridge line
  • Washroom door narrower than the front door
  • Washstand and basin; no bath, no shower and no grab rail

A guest who cannot manage the ladder stair can still take the cabin: the box bed off the porch room sleeps two on the entrance floor, and we will make it up in preference to the loft if you say so when you book. In that case the cabin sleeps two rather than four.

Light, heat and water

Lighting is oil lantern and candle throughout, so there is no switch to find in the dark and nothing at all outside. Bring a torch each. Heat is the wood stove, which needs laying, lighting and feeding through the evening; it is the only heating in the building, and the cabin cools quickly once it goes out. Water is one cold tap at the sink, drawn from the spring behind the woodshed. Hot water comes from the kettle on the stove, and the sauna hut on the tarn shore, thirty metres down a stepped path, is where guests wash properly. Carrying water and carrying wood are both part of staying here.

Help, and how far away it is

The gate lodge is four kilometres down the track and attended mornings. There is no telephone line on the ridge and no mobile signal past the quarry turning, so an emergency means driving or walking down to Fernlee. A first-aid box hangs inside the pantry door. Assistance dogs are welcome anywhere on the ridge, and the tarn shore path is the one route we would call easy going. Guests who need medication kept cold should know that the pantry is a cold slab rather than a refrigerator.
