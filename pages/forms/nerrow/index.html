The twenty-second meeting

Four days of plenaries, contributed talks and shore work at Weyburn Point Marine Station, 3 to 6 November 2026.

The symposium has met at the narrow end of the Nerrow Strait since the station's first winter survey, and it keeps the shape those early meetings settled on: one plenary each morning, parallel sessions of short contributed talks through the afternoon, and a working walk on the foreshore whenever the tide allows one.

Delegates come from coastal institutes, fisheries offices, records cooperatives and the volunteer survey groups that have counted this shoreline for decades. The meeting is deliberately small, so a student presenting a first season of data reads to the same room as the panel chairs.

What the meeting covers

Eight sessions run over the four days, from tidal hydrodynamics and sediment transport through invertebrates of the low shore, seabird foraging, fish movement, seagrass, saltmarsh and nearshore macroalgae. The full list of sessions and their chairs is on the sessions page, and the day-by-day outline is on the programme.

Contributed slots are fifteen minutes plus questions, and the poster corridor stays open through the middle two days. Studies are placed in sessions by the reviewer panel rather than chosen by their authors, which is what keeps the parallel sessions coherent for delegates moving between them.

Plenary lectures

  • Prof. Maren Aldous, Carrickmoor University, opens the meeting on the Tuesday with eight decades of shoreline notebooks read as a single archive.
  • Dr. Tobias Reyner, Halden Sound Institute of Coastal Studies, on sediment memory in a narrow channel.
  • Dr. Sylvie Okafor, Northern Channel Observatory, on survey design for tidal passages.
  • Prof. Callum Herriot, Weyburn Point Marine Station, closes with what the strait should be measuring by the thirtieth meeting.

Coming to Weyburn Point

The station stands on Cobble Quay, a short walk from the ferry steps. November sailings from Carrickmoor harbour are tide-limited and the connecting coach from Halden meets only two arrivals a day, so settle the crossing before booking a room. Crossings, lodging and the step-free route into the hall are all set out on the travel and lodging page.

The desk answers registration and programme questions on weekdays between 09:00 and 16:00 at desk@nerrowstrait.example. Delegates who need a letter of invitation for a visa application should write early, since the desk issues them in the order they are asked for.
