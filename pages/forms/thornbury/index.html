The Thornbury Trust

An independent grant-making trust funding coastal and marine ecology in the United Kingdom and Ireland

Who we are

The Thornbury Trust was established in 1978 from the estate of a Bristol shipping family and has funded field ecology on and around the coast ever since. We are a small endowed trust with a part-time grants office and a volunteer assessment panel drawn from university departments, wildlife trusts and harbour authorities.

Our awards are deliberately modest and deliberately quick. Most of the work we support is a season of survey or sampling that would otherwise never be costed into a larger programme: a stretch of estuary counted properly, a saltmarsh transect repeated after twenty years, a species record confirmed rather than assumed. We fund the people already standing on the shore.

Applying for a grant

Applications are made online through the applicant portal, which holds your draft while you work on it and lets you follow it once it reaches the review queue. Read the applicant guidance first: it sets out what the current round funds, what it will not fund, and how budgets should be presented.

What we look for

A strong application answers a question that can be settled with the time and money requested. The panel is more persuaded by a narrow question and a credible method than by an ambitious one and an optimistic timetable. Site access matters as much as method: an application that names its sites and explains how permission will be secured is markedly more likely to be funded.

We welcome applications from early-career researchers, independent ecologists and county recording groups as readily as from university departments. A host organisation is required only where the grant needs to be held by one.

Governance and reporting

Trustees meet four times a year and publish the annual report and accounts alongside a list of every award made in the preceding year. The assessment panel is refreshed on a three-year rotation, and panellists declare and stand aside from any application connected to their own institution.

Grant holders report once at the halfway point and once on completion. We ask for a short account of what was found, including what did not work, and we ask that any resulting data be lodged with an appropriate public record centre.
