Code of conduct

Applies to Delegates, speakers, exhibitors, crew Where All five halls and the terrace

The summit works because six hundred people say what they actually think in rooms small enough to be heard in. That only holds while everybody is safe to speak, which is what this code is for.

What we expect

  • Argue with the idea, not the person holding it.
  • Leave room in a session for the people who have not spoken yet.
  • Treat a story told in a roundtable as told in confidence unless the teller says otherwise.
  • Ask before photographing anyone, and never during a roundtable.
  • Take a chair's ruling on time and turn-taking without relitigating it.
  • Follow crew instructions in the halls, on the terrace and at the loading doors.

What ends a badge

Harassment of any kind, including sustained personal attacks, unwanted physical contact, slurs, and following or photographing someone who has asked you to stop. Intimidation of a speaker, a chair or a member of crew. Bringing a recording device into a roundtable. Circulating anything said in a closed session outside it.

None of these need to be repeated to be acted on. A single incident is enough for the summit to withdraw a badge, and a withdrawn badge is not refunded.

Recording and attribution

Keynotes and panels

Photography is fine, and quoting a speaker with attribution is welcome. Video and audio recording need the speaker's agreement and the chair's.

Roundtables and workshops

No recording of any kind, and nothing attributed outside the room. Take the idea home with you; leave the name and the employer behind.

Raising something

In the moment Find any member of crew. Crew wear violet lanyards and are posted at every hall entrance, and they are briefed to act rather than to refer you onward.

At the desk The delegation desk in Hall C takes reports in person all three days, and by email at groups@solstice-events.example before and after the summit.

Quietly The quiet lounge in Hall E has a duty organiser on call through each day for anyone who would rather not raise something at a busy desk.

How a report is handled

Two organisers hear every report, and one of them is always someone with no connection to the session or the delegation involved. You will be told what has been decided and why, in the same conversation wherever the facts allow it.

The summit can move a seat, reassign a roundtable, ask someone to leave a session, or withdraw a badge for the remainder of the three days. Reports are held by the organiser only, are not shared with anyone's employer, and are destroyed once the summit closes unless a badge was withdrawn.

Delegations register on behalf of their people, so a withdrawn badge is reported to whoever submitted the delegation at registration and to nobody else.
