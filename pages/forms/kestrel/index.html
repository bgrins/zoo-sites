How we build

A Kestrel Peak trailer begins as a folded aluminum monocoque, cut on a jig we made ourselves and welded in one piece. Nothing structural is bolted on afterwards, which is why the frame carries a six-year warranty and why a fifteen-year-old shell still closes squarely on its hinges.

The cabins are finished in birch, sealed rather than laminated, so a scuff sands out instead of peeling. Wiring runs in labeled looms behind removable panels, and the diagram that matches your build sheet ships with the trailer. Owners who service their own trailers tell us that this is the part they notice second, after the tow manners.

We build to order rather than to a lot, so a spec stays open until the frame is cut. That keeps the shop small and the lead time honest: fourteen to twenty-two weeks depending on chassis, with a build week confirmed about a month ahead.
