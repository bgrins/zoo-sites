Where work gets done

Bearings, brakes, seals, hinges and electrics are ordinary trailer work, and any competent shop can do them on a Kestrel Peak. What we ask is that anything structural comes to us or to a dealer, because a monocoque shell is repaired by a welder who knows where the load paths run.

The Switchback Road service bay takes bookings Tuesday to Saturday and keeps two slots a week clear for owners passing through on a trip. Our five dealers carry the common consumables and handle warranty inspections, so a bearing that starts making noise in Colorado does not need to travel to Oregon first.

Booking the shop bay

  • Call the shop with your VIN. The build sheet tells us which running gear, galley and solar package we are looking at before you arrive.
  • Routine work is quoted before it starts, and quoted again if we find something the walk-around missed.
  • Annual seal and roof inspections take half a day; a bearing repack and suspension check takes a full one.
  • Structural assessments are booked for a whole day, since the shell comes apart far enough to see the welds.
  • Overnight parking on the lot is fine for owners who have driven a long way. Tell us when you book so the gate stays open.

Parts by mail

Parts ship from the shop, usually the same week. Call or write with your VIN rather than a part description: the three chassis share most of their hardware but not all of it, and the wrong hinge costs you two weeks.

Older trailers

We hold shell hardware for every model year we have built, back to the first trailers to leave the shop in 2011. Where a supplier has stopped making a bracket we fabricate a replacement on the original drawing, which takes longer but keeps a fifteen-year-old trailer closing squarely on its hinges.

Wiring diagrams, torque figures and the sealant specification for your build are all on the printed manual that shipped with the trailer, and the shop will mail a replacement copy free of charge. Warranty coverage and the service interval table sit on the owners page.
