Advisory levels

Every destination advisory carries a level from 1 to 4. The level is the Authority's standing assessment of conditions for travellers and is settled at the rolling six-week review.

The scale

Level 1 — Take normal precautions. Conditions are broadly comparable to those in settled parts of the region. Follow the standing guidance for the destination and keep travel documents secure.

Level 2 — Exercise increased caution. There are known risks that careful planning can manage. Read the standing guidance closely and check for supplementary notices before and during travel.

Level 3 — Reconsider travel. Serious risks affect much of the destination. Travel only for essential purposes, make contingency plans, and tell the duty officer or your consulate about your itinerary.

Level 4 — Do not travel. Conditions present a severe risk to life or liberty, or the Authority cannot support travellers in the area. Travellers already present should leave when it is safe to do so.

How levels are set

Levels are proposed by the regional desks and settled at the six-week review. A level describes the destination as a whole; conditions in particular districts may be better or worse than the level suggests, and the standing guidance notes such differences.

Levels and supplementary notices

A supplementary notice issued between reviews may impose specific restrictions or advice without changing the level. Always read the notices on a destination's advisory page together with its level.
