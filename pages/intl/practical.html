Practical information

Entry, health, legal, and financial guidance for travellers to the Qandara administrative region. Read this page alongside the advisory for your destination.

Entry and exit requirements

Most visitors need a visa, issued by Qandara consulates abroad; some nationalities can obtain one on arrival at Marsat Qandara airport. Check with the nearest consulate well before travelling.

Travel documents must be valid for at least six months beyond the intended date of departure and have one blank page for stamps. Keep the entry stamp legible; an unclear or missing stamp can delay departure while the record is verified.

Travellers staying longer than 90 days need a long-stay visa arranged before arrival. Overstays attract a fine collected at departure.

Health and vaccination

No vaccinations are required for entry, but routine immunisations should be up to date. Hospitals in Marsat Qandara handle most treatment; facilities elsewhere are limited, and serious cases are moved to the capital.

Tap water is not reliably potable outside Marsat Qandara. Carry any regular medication in its original packaging with a copy of the prescription.

Local laws and customs

Dress modestly at religious sites and in the older districts. Penalties for possession of narcotics are severe. Photography of government and harbour installations requires written permission from the responsible office.

The weekly rest day is Friday; government offices and many businesses also close on Saturday.

Money and banking

The currency is the Qandara dinar. The region is largely a cash economy; cards are accepted at larger hotels in Marsat Qandara and rarely elsewhere. Cash machines are found in the capital and in district towns, and may be empty before the Friday rest day.

Banks open Sunday to Thursday, mornings only. Exchange money only at banks and licensed bureaux and keep the receipts, which may be requested at departure.

Customs allowances

Visitors aged 18 and over may bring in, free of duty: 200 cigarettes or 250 grams of tobacco; one litre of spirits or two litres of wine; and personal effects, including one camera and one portable computer, for their own use.

Currency above 10,000 dinars or its equivalent must be declared on arrival and departure. Exporting antiquities requires a licence from the heritage office; unlicensed export is a criminal offence.
