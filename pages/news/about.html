About millrace

Millrace is a link stream run by and for people who build things: software, hardware, instruments, businesses, the occasional shed. Members submit links and questions; everyone votes and argues; the stream re-ranks itself every ten minutes. Forty-one thousand people read it; a few thousand write.

It is operated by Weirgate Collective LLC, a two-letter publisher in Ashvale, Vermont. Our other letter is Grindstone Weekly, a printed column on running small companies; one Grindstone essay a week is set for the web and shared on the stream.

How the ranking works

A submission's position is a function of three things:

  1. Votes. One member, one vote per submission. Votes from accounts younger than a week count at half weight.
  2. Reply pace. A thread gathering thoughtful replies rises faster than one gathering only votes. Reply pace is measured over the last two hours, so an old thread that wakes up can climb back.
  3. Decay. Everything sinks with age. Most submissions get a day or two on the front pages; after that they drop off the stream entirely and live on at their thread's address.

Flags work against all three: a submission with credible flags falls fast and shows up in the moderation log when the stewards act on it.

What the numbers mean

The figure in a submission's pill is its points: votes minus flags-upheld. The reply count next to the byline counts every reply in the thread, including replies to replies; the thread page nests them under their parents so you can tell conversation from chorus.

Sponsored listings

Two slots a day are sold to companies that are hiring. Each is marked with a hiring chip instead of a points pill, never ranks above where it was placed, and takes no replies. Details on the sponsor a day page.

Who runs this

Three stewards, all working engineers, moderate part-time under the published guidelines. Everything they remove or demote lands in the public moderation log. Questions go to stewards@millrace.example.

House rules

  • Reply to the strongest version of the argument.
  • Say who you work for when it is relevant.
  • Title your submission the way the author would.
  • One account per person, one vote per submission.
