About this page

What this page covers, where its numbers come from, and who updates it during an incident.

What this page reports

This page reports the health of the five Nimbrel Edge components a customer can observe directly: edge delivery, the relay mesh, the API gateway, the control panel, and log streaming. Each component is scored by the synthetic probes that run against it from twenty-two vantage points, once a minute, over the same public routes customer traffic takes. A component moves off Operational when probes from at least three vantage points agree that its service objective is being missed, which keeps a single unhappy vantage point from raising a platform-wide alarm.

Anything outside those five components is out of scope here. Billing, the partner portal, and the marketing site have their own owners and are not scored on this page, so a fault in one of them can leave every component here green. Individual customer accounts are also out of scope: a limit reached on your own account is not a platform incident, and the control panel is the place to see it.

Who updates it

The network operations center owns this page and staffs it continuously. The engineer holding the incident opens the record, writes every update on it, and closes it; nothing on this page is written by an automated summarizer, which is why an update can lag detection by a few minutes and why the wording changes between updates as understanding improves.

Our commitment during an incident is an opening note within fifteen minutes of the component changing state, and an update at least every thirty minutes after that until the incident is resolved. A resolved incident stays on the page. We do not delete incident records, and we do not edit an earlier update once it has been published; a correction arrives as a new update that says what the earlier one got wrong.

Relay path checks

The relay path check on the status page is a different instrument from the component scores. It runs from your network rather than from our vantage points, so it can report a congested path while the mesh is scored Operational everywhere else. Each check is assigned a probe code that identifies it in our logs for seven days. Quoting that code to support saves an engineer from asking you to reproduce the problem.

The check is read-only and adds no measurable load. It does not feed the trailing uptime figures, and running it repeatedly will not change them.

Reaching a person

If what you are seeing does not match this page, tell us rather than waiting for it to catch up. Write to the network operations center with the affected component, the region you are served from, and a probe code if you have one.
